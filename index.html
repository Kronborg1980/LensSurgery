The hospital

Vijaya Nethralaya Super Speciality Eye Hospital

Vijaya Nethralaya is a dedicated eye hospital in Nagarbhavi, on the western side of Bengaluru. Eyes are all it does. Under one roof it runs cataract and lens surgery, LASIK and other laser vision correction, implantable lenses (ICL), retina, glaucoma, cornea and transplants, children's eye care, oculoplasty and dry-eye treatment. It also has its own optical shop, an eye bank and an optometry institute.

The hospital is chaired by Dr. Appaji Gowda, a senior cataract surgeon and former head of department at Kempegowda Institute of Medical Sciences. Its day-to-day leader is his colleague Dr. Sushruth Appaji Gowda, who is both CEO and chief cataract and refractive surgeon. Around them is a team of sub-specialists in retina, glaucoma, cornea and paediatric eye care, so anything found during your pre-operative tests can be assessed in the same building.

For travellers, the useful part is the hospital's medical tourism programme. It says it can arrange airport pickup and drop from Kempegowda International Airport, transfers between your hotel and the hospital, hotel booking, a private air-conditioned car for getting around, meals to suit your diet, and sightseeing once the surgeon says you are ready. Ask for this when you first get in touch.

What you can have done

Lens replacement, laser or an implantable lens

There are three main routes to living without glasses. Which one fits depends on your age, your prescription, the thickness and shape of your cornea and the health of the rest of your eye. The surgeon decides that after the tests, so travel with an open mind about the method.

Lens replacement

The eye's natural lens is removed and an artificial intraocular lens (IOL) is placed in the same capsule. It is the same operation as cataract surgery. It suits people from their mid-forties upward, people losing near vision, and anyone with an early cataract. It is permanent, and you can never develop a cataract afterwards.

Laser correction

LASIK, SMILE and surface treatments such as PRK reshape the cornea with a laser. They suit younger adults with a stable prescription and a healthy cornea. The hospital offers wavefront-guided LASIK and SMILE. Recovery is fast, often a day or two for LASIK.

Implantable lens (ICL)

A thin lens is placed in front of your natural lens, which stays where it is. It is used for high prescriptions or corneas too thin for laser, and it can be removed again later.

The lens I chose: Rayner RayOne Galaxy Toric

Rayner is the British company that made the world's first intraocular lens in 1949. The RayOne Galaxy was launched in September 2024 and went on general release in 2025. Rayner describes it as the first spiral IOL, with an optic shaped using an AI engine trained on patient outcomes.

Most multifocal lenses split light with fine rings, which costs some light and can cause halos around lamps at night. The Galaxy uses a smooth spiral instead. According to Rayner it gives a continuous range of focus from far to near with no loss of transmitted light, and in a small pre-clinical study its halo was closer in size to that of a single-focus lens than to a ring-based trifocal.

Toric means the lens also corrects astigmatism. It has to sit at an exact angle in the eye, which is one reason the measurements before surgery are so careful.

Good to know before you choose

  • No premium lens suits every eye. Retina, cornea and dry-eye findings can change the recommendation.
  • Some people still notice glare or halos at night, or want light reading glasses for small print.
  • Any eye surgery carries risk, including infection and retinal problems. Ask the surgeon what the risks are for your eyes.
  • Your brain needs weeks to months to adapt fully to a new optic.

The trip in eight steps

Do these in order. Each one depends on the one before it, and the first is the one people skip: talk to the hospital before you book anything.

  1. Contact the hospitalCall or message, say you are an international patient, and send your current glasses prescription and any eye history. Ask which treatment they expect to suit you and how many days you should stay.
  2. Agree dates and get the letterAsk for a letter on the hospital's letterhead with your name, nationality, passport number and the planned treatment date. You need it for the e-Medical visa.
  3. Stop wearing contact lensesContacts change the shape of the cornea and spoil the measurements. Ask the hospital how long before the tests you must stop. Hard lenses need much longer than soft ones.
  4. Apply for the visaApply on the official Indian government portal at least 4 days and at most 120 days before you arrive.
  5. Book flights to BengaluruFly into BLR. Keep the return flexible or changeable in case the surgeon wants an extra check.
  6. Book the hotel and the airport pickupChoose a quiet room on the hospital's side of the city, or let the hospital arrange both.
  7. Fill in the e-Arrival cardComplete it online within the 72 hours before you land.
  8. Arrange a check at homeBook an eye examination with your own optician or ophthalmologist for a few weeks after you return, and bring your surgery papers.

Paperwork

The visa: apply online for an e-Medical visa

Citizens of more than 170 countries, including all of the EU, the UK, the USA, Canada and Australia, can get an Indian e-Visa online. You do not visit an embassy. The category made for treatment is the e-Medical visa. A companion applies for an e-Medical Attendant visa; up to two attendants are allowed per patient.

Apply only at the government site, indianvisaonline.gov.in. Many lookalike sites charge extra for the same form. The government says there is no express fee and no agent is needed.

You fill in the form, upload your documents, pay by card and receive an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) by email. Print it. At immigration in Bengaluru your fingerprints and photo are taken and the visa is stamped in your passport.

The portal's guidance gives the e-Medical visa 60 days from first arrival with repeat entries inside that period. The fee depends on your nationality and is not refunded. Rules and fees change, so read the portal's current text before you apply.

Arriving

Fly to Kempegowda International Airport, code BLR

Bengaluru has one airport, and it is an approved entry point for e-Visa holders. All international flights use Terminal 2, a new building filled with plants and bamboo. There are direct flights from the Gulf hubs, Singapore, and several European cities, so most travellers reach it with one change at most.

The airport is about 40 km north of the city centre. The hospital is on the far western side, so allow one and a half to two hours by road, and more in the evening rush. The easiest arrival is the hospital's own pickup: a driver waits for you and takes you to your hotel.

If you arrange it yourself, use the official prepaid taxi counter inside arrivals for a fixed fare, or book an Uber or Ola and follow the signs to the app-cab pickup zone. Ignore anyone who approaches you offering a taxi in the hall.

Where to sleep

Pick the hotel for recovery, then for sightseeing

Bengaluru traffic is slow, and you will visit the hospital several times with dilated or freshly operated eyes. Distance to the hospital matters more than being near the famous sights. You have three sensible choices.

Let the hospital book it

The medical tourism team books hotels and resorts and ties them to your transfers. This is the least work, and the driver already knows your appointment times.

International hotels on the west side

Rajajinagar and Malleshwaram are on the hospital's side of the city and have full-service hotels, including Sheraton Grand Bangalore at Brigade Gateway and Fairfield by Marriott Rajajinagar. Both sit beside shopping and restaurants.

Classic city-centre luxury

Taj West End and The Oberoi are the names the hospital itself suggests. They are beautiful garden hotels in the centre. The price is a longer drive to each appointment.

How to find a good one yourself

  • Open the hospital's map pin and search hotels around it. Check the drive time at 9 in the morning and 6 in the evening.
  • Filter for a guest score of 8 out of 10 or higher and read the most recent reviews first.
  • Ask for a quiet room away from the road, with blackout curtains and air conditioning you control.
  • Choose a hotel with room service and a restaurant, so you can eat without going out on surgery days.
  • Book a rate you can cancel or extend, in case your dates move.

A resort for the last days

Once the surgeon agrees, a few nights in the green outskirts is a good way to rest. The hospital lists these: Ranch at Big Banyan Vineyard, Mango Mist, The Windflower, Karma and Evolve Back.

Practical

Getting around and day-to-day details

Transport

Uber and Ola work across the city and are cheap by European standards. Do not drive yourself, and do not ride a scooter or an open auto-rickshaw with a healing eye: the dust is the problem. The Namma Metro purple line serves the west side at Vijayanagar and Attiguppe.

Money

The currency is the Indian rupee (₹, INR). Hotels, the hospital and larger restaurants take international cards. Carry some cash for small shops and tips. ATMs are everywhere. Ask the hospital how it prefers to be paid before you travel.

Phone and internet

Install an eSIM before you fly, or buy a local SIM at the airport with your passport. Hotel Wi-Fi is normally good. Save the hospital's numbers and your hotel address offline.

Language

The local language is Kannada. English is spoken at the hospital, in hotels and by most drivers, so you will manage easily.

Weather

Bengaluru sits about 900 metres above sea level, so it is milder than most of India. November to February is dry and pleasant. June to October is the rainy season. April and May are the hottest.

Power

230 volts. Sockets take the round-pin types C, D and M, so a European two-pin plug usually fits. Bring a universal adapter to be sure.

Insurance

Ordinary travel insurance rarely covers planned surgery or its complications. Read the policy, tell the insurer why you are travelling, and consider a medical-travel policy.

Health

See your own doctor or a travel clinic a few weeks before you go about vaccinations. Bring a list of your medicines and tell the surgeon about every one, including blood thinners.

The cuisine

Eat South Indian, and eat carefully before surgery

Bengaluru is the home of South Indian breakfast. The food is rice and lentil based, mostly vegetarian, and far milder than people expect. Start the day with a crisp masala dosa, soft idli with coconut chutney, or a savoury vada dipped in sambar, and finish with filter coffee served in a steel tumbler.

For lunch, try a Karnataka thali: a round tray of rice, vegetable dishes, lentils, yoghurt and a sweet. Bisi bele bath is the local comfort dish, a hot rice and lentil stew. Ragi mudde, a ball of finger-millet dough eaten with curry, is the food of the countryside. Mysore pak is the sweet to bring home.

The city's old dosa houses are institutions: MTR (Mavalli Tiffin Rooms) near Lalbagh, Vidyarthi Bhavan in Basavanagudi, and CTR in Malleshwaram. Bengaluru is also India's craft beer and cafe capital, with every world cuisine available when you want a break from spice.

Keep your stomach safe

  • A stomach bug can postpone your operation. Be strict until surgery is done.
  • Drink sealed bottled water. Skip ice and tap water, and use bottled water for brushing teeth.
  • Eat food that is cooked and served hot. Busy places with fast turnover are the safest.
  • Avoid raw salads, cut fruit and street juices. Fruit you peel yourself is fine.
  • Say "less spicy" when you order. Kitchens are used to it.
  • Wash or sanitise your hands before eating. You will also be touching your face to put in drops.

After the operation

Recovery, and what to see while you heal

Looking after the eye

  • Use the drops exactly on the schedule you are given. Set alarms on your phone.
  • Do not rub or press the eye. Wear the shield at night for as long as you are told.
  • Wear sunglasses outdoors. Light will feel bright and the city is dusty.
  • Keep water, soap and shampoo out of the eye. No swimming pools or spa until cleared.
  • No heavy lifting, bending low or hard exercise in the first days. No eye make-up.
  • Ask the surgeon directly when you may fly, and take enough drops for the journey in your hand luggage.

Call the hospital at once if you get increasing pain, a sudden drop in vision, a shower of new floaters, flashes of light, or a red eye that is getting worse. At home, go to an eye emergency service the same day.

Gentle outings in Bengaluru

Lalbagh Botanical Garden and Cubbon Park are shady and calm. Bangalore Palace and the ISKCON temple are easy half-day visits by car. Nandi Hills is the classic sunrise drive.

Further out, once you are cleared

Mysuru, about 140 km away, has the Mysore Palace and Brindavan Gardens. The Hoysala temples at Belur, Halebidu and Somanathapura have extraordinary stone carving. Coorg offers coffee plantations, and Kabini offers elephant and tiger safaris. Bannerghatta Biological Park is only 25 km from the city. The hospital arranges cars and packages for all of these.
