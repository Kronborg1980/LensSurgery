What you can have done

Lens replacement, laser or an implantable lens

There are three main routes to life without glasses. Which one fits depends on your age, your prescription, your cornea and the health of the rest of your eye. The surgeon decides after the tests, so travel with an open mind.

    Before you book

    Have your eyes checked at home first, and know the risks

    Not every eye suits a premium lens like the RayOne Galaxy Toric. Before you book flights, have a full eye examination at home with an ophthalmologist (eye doctor). Ask for a copy of the results and send it to the hospital, so Dr. Sushruth can tell you in advance whether the lens you want is likely to suit you.

    The final decision is still made after the detailed measurements in Bengaluru. Agree a plan B before you travel, for example a different type of lens, so a change on the day does not come as a shock.

    Conditions that can change the choice of lens

    • Macular degeneration or other disease of the macula
    • Diabetic changes in the retina
    • Glaucoma with damage to the optic nerve
    • Keratoconus or an irregular cornea
    • Earlier laser eye surgery, which makes the lens calculation harder
    • Marked dry eye, which should be treated first
    • A lazy eye (amblyopia)

    What the examination at home should cover

    • Your full glasses prescription, including astigmatism
    • The health of the retina and the macula, the centre of sharp vision
    • Eye pressure and the optic nerve, to rule out glaucoma
    • The shape of the cornea (topography) and whether it is regular
    • The tear film, to check for dry eye
    • Any earlier eye surgery or laser treatment, with the records

    Risks and side effects to know about

    Lens surgery is one of the most common operations in the world, and most people have no serious problems. These are the risks to understand before you decide. Ask the surgeon how they apply to your eyes.

    Clouding behind the lens (PCO)

    Months or years after surgery, the thin capsule that holds the new lens can turn cloudy and vision becomes hazy again. It is often called a secondary cataract and is one of the most common after-effects. It is fixed with a quick, painless YAG laser treatment, which an eye clinic at home can usually do. Ask your home clinic what it costs.

    Dry eyes

    Dry, gritty or burning eyes are common in the first weeks and can last a few months. Lubricating eye drops help. If you already have dry eyes, have them treated before surgery, because that also makes the measurements more accurate.

    Halos and glare at night

    Rings or starbursts around lights can appear, especially with lenses that give a range of focus. They usually fade as the brain adapts over weeks to months, but for some people they remain.

    Inflammation and eye pressure

    Some inflammation and a rise in eye pressure are common in the first days and are controlled with the drops you are given. This is why the check-ups before you fly home matter.

    Result off target or lens rotation

    The result can end up slightly off, or a toric lens can turn out of position. You may then need glasses for some tasks, an adjustment of the lens or a laser touch-up.

    Swelling at the centre of the retina

    Some weeks after surgery, fluid can collect in the macula and blur central vision. It is uncommon and is usually treated with eye drops.

    Retinal detachment

    Rare, but more likely in very short-sighted eyes, and it can happen long after surgery. New flashes, a shower of floaters or a shadow or curtain in your vision need an eye doctor the same day.

    Infection

    Very rare but serious. Increasing pain, redness and falling vision in the first days need urgent help. This is why the drops and good hygiene matter so much.

    Arrange follow-up at home before you leave. Find an eye clinic at home that will see you after the trip, can treat problems such as PCO or dry eye, and will contact the hospital in India if needed. Bring all your surgery papers, including the exact lens model and power.
